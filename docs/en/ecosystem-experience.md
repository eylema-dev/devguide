# Private learning and eligible ecosystem experience

Eylema is an ecosystem-backed agent platform: interchangeable local open-weight specialists cooperate through owned Rust engines, stable role interfaces and harness tools. Completed-job quality, resource efficiency, privacy boundaries and repeatable improvement are measured together. Controlled development of Eylema itself uses maintainer-qualified powerful LLM contribution and genuine separate AI review under owner task/merge authority. Discussion, ideas and bug reports remain open. Local product use does not require an external large model; explicitly configured external roles need their own data/call authority.

This is the target design and acceptance contract, not a claim that all inference, model-specific training, RAG or institutional deployment is implemented. [Current action plan](platform-action-plan.md), [resource advisor](model-resource-planning.md) and [contribution policy](contribution-model-policy.md) bind the implementation gates.

## Two destinations for accumulated value

| Destination | Eligible contents | Required boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private project or institution | Permitted observations, corrections, knowledge, evaluation and learning derivatives for scoped memory/RAG/fine-tune | Private by default; project/organization/purpose access, retention and collect/read/retrieve/train permissions enforced; no ecosystem export implied |
| Reviewed ecosystem candidate | Generalized, anonymization-reviewed lessons, recipes, model/profile selections, synthetic or otherwise expressly shareable data, evaluations and resource measurements | Separate disclosure/export/publication authority, source and derivative rights, provenance and re-identification review before repository admission |

A record may remain private forever and still help the owner's local system improve. Open model weights do not make task data, RAG sources, prompts, corrections, embeddings, adapters or trained derivatives public. Publishing Eylema source does not authorize publication of its users' records. A package candidate is not publication, and package import is not execution or project access.

## Improvement loop

1. Capture only permitted typed outcomes: proposal versus attempted tool action versus observed result, human correction, failure and unknown. Record task/model/profile/recipe versions and evidence status; exclude raw conversations, private reasoning, credentials and restricted content from ecosystem candidates.
2. Attach source, purpose, collection/retrieval/training/disclosure/export rights, scope, retention and withdrawal lineage. Every derived summary, embedding, dataset, adapter or recipe example retains its restrictions.
3. Curate private scoped memory/RAG and model-specific training inputs. Keep episode families and derivatives together during train/holdout splitting; external-model suggestions are distinct from measured outcomes and human decisions.
4. Evaluate candidate improvements against immutable holdouts and full-loop quality/resource/privacy gates. Native Rust operator/profile support, canary and rollback evidence precede activation; a new model name, popular recipe or catalog import cannot bypass them.
5. If separately permitted, generalize a bounded ecosystem candidate. Remove or transform identifying fields and examine indirect identifiers, rare events, linkage and reconstruction risks. Pseudonymization/redaction alone does not prove anonymity; missing rights or unresolved risk keeps the candidate ineligible.
6. Review the exact package, lineage, terms, expiry/revocation state and intended destination. Actual authorized publication follows review; imports preserve restrictions and stay inert until compatible reviewed activation. Feed verified permitted lessons back into source development and local use.

Models and execution details may evolve; role semantics, authority boundaries, provenance and evidence rules remain versioned. A future institutional profile must prove its own isolation and operating environment; a local console test is not organizational deployment acceptance.

## Planned Goal acceptance

| Goal | Additional evidence required |
| --- | --- |
| G33 episodes | Separate source-development and in-product lessons, observed/suggested/corrected status, private scopes and collect/retrieve/train/disclose/export permission failures |
| G34 dataset revision | Private institutional RAG/training paths, inherited derivative restrictions, withdrawal and related-episode holdout isolation; no automatic public conversion |
| G64 share candidate | Synthetic direct/indirect identifier and linkage failures, separate export/publication rights, package provenance and explicit uncertain anonymity rejection |
| G65 ecosystem admission | Exact reviewed package identity/terms/lineage, compatibility and revoked/expired/mismatched rights rejection; no automatic download/execute/train/activate |

Withdrawal stops future use/admission and propagates through controlled local derivatives. For already public Git packages and external copies, policy must describe reachable removals and remaining exposure honestly; it cannot promise recall of all clones. Public repositories hold only reviewed eligible packages/recipes/metadata under explicit destination rules, never a default dump of user activity.

No user data is collected, anonymized, exported or published by this documentation revision. G33/G34/G64/G65 remain planned; accepted G63 local boundary evidence remains within its tested scope. Existing dependencies, budgets, accepted history and blocked G40 allowances are unchanged.
